The headline measure is simple: ridership went up. The useful question is what changed underneath that total.

After the pandemic, İzmir’s transport modes returned at different speeds. Buses carried the most trips, while some rail modes climbed faster against their January 2021 baseline. Passenger groups also returned on different timelines.

The 3D map connects municipal ridership data to the network’s geography. It compares volume, recovery pace, and passenger mix on the same monthly timeline.

## How to read the map

The scene uses three visual signals:

- **Density:** Particle count represents monthly trips.
- **Speed:** Particle speed represents recovery against January 2021 (100).
- **Color:** Each transport mode keeps one color; the panel on the right lists riders by fare group.

## What stands out

1. **Volume and pace are different measures.** Buses carry the most trips, while Metro and İZBAN rise more sharply against their own baseline.
2. **Student travel is seasonal.** The passenger mix changes in clear steps rather than one uniform return.

## Why use a map?

İzmir’s bay shapes the network: ferries cross it, rail runs along a north-south spine, trams follow the coast, and buses connect the wider districts.

Projecting the routes into a local Three.js scene keeps that geography visible while the timeline changes.
