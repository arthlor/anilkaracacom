Every day, thousands of commuters, public transit riders, and logistics workers navigate Izmir’s arterial corridors. But their journeys are shaped by an invisible map of risk. By examining over 17,000 traffic incident records from the Izmir Traffic Center spanning more than two years, we reveal that traffic delays and collisions are not random mishaps—they are the predictable results of structural bottlenecks and systemic pressure points. This data tells the story of who bears the burden of road safety, where the city's infrastructure fractures under peak demand, and when risk spikes for everyday citizens.

## Civic Safety and Infrastructure Equity: Key Takeaways

Urban mobility is a public good, but its risks are unevenly shared. Analyzing Izmir’s traffic incident records yields critical lessons for civic planners, transit advocates, and residents:

1. **Prioritize Fragile Corridors over General Expansion**
   Instead of widening roads, municipal efforts should focus on safety retrofits and transit priority lanes on structurally vulnerable corridors like **Yeşildere Caddesi** and **Anadolu Caddesi**. These corridors consistently rank as the highest-risk zones year after year, suggesting that their current layout is fundamentally incompatible with safe speed limits and mixed traffic.

2. **The Economic Cost of Transit Disruptions**
   With thousands of mechanical breakdowns (*Arızalı*) and minor collisions blocking roads each year, the primary victim of traffic congestion is often the bus commuter. When a single lane on a major boulevard is blocked, it delays public transit lines, disproportionately affecting shift workers who lack private vehicles. Expanding rapid-response towing services and establishing bus-only lanes can mitigate this hidden economic tax.

3. **Targeted Peak-Hour Interventions**
   The dramatic concentration of incidents during Friday evening commute hours suggests that safety campaigns, traffic enforcement, and emergency responder staging should be dynamically adjusted. Rather than uniform city-wide patrol schedules, resources should be concentrated at known bottlenecks during the high-density Friday 17:00–19:00 window.
